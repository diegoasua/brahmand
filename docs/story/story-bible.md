# Asteria story bible

Status: early narrative foundation, 2026-08-01.

This file records the decisions that should remain stable while individual chapters, dialogue, and assets evolve.

## Canon

- The story begins in **2147**.
- **Asteria** is a human-built, AI-powered science-exploration spacecraft designed to preserve and teach scientific knowledge.
- The player directly navigates Asteria. There is no embodied pilot, walkable interior, or separate interior map.
- The human playing the game is the learner; there does not need to be a fictional student avatar aboard the ship.
- **AURA** is Asteria's surviving guidance and knowledge interface. AURA can diagnose systems, teach, ask questions, and speak, but its damaged memory is incomplete.
- Play happens in one seamless space-navigation world. Nearby regions may eventually stream behind the scenes, but the player does not enter separate maps.
- Celestial destinations use real astronomical names. Physical facts about them must be sourced and reviewed.
- Artistic rendering scale is distinct from physical scale. The interface must not present displayed distances, sizes, or travel times as real measurements.
- Fictional infrastructure—future stations, probes, archives, and machines—is allowed when it is clearly presented as story fiction.
- The game has narrative progress rather than points, scores, or punishment for incorrect answers.

## Science contract

Every educational encounter separates three kinds of information:

1. **Reviewed science**: sourced claims about nature, materials, or technology.
2. **Engineering scenario**: invented constraints that make a mission solvable, such as a connector size or available repair cartridge.
3. **Story fiction**: characters, future infrastructure, the memory failure, and the larger mystery.

An LLM may rephrase approved material and use well-established general science to answer open-ended conversation. It may not invent a fact needed to complete a quest, present uncertainty as settled science, or silently turn story fiction into a scientific claim. Reviewed entries remain the source of quick facts, and quest correctness remains deterministic.

Incorrect answers should reveal a useful distinction. A player who selects a material that conducts electricity but does not satisfy the mission's engineering constraints has demonstrated partial understanding and should be told so.

## Voice and input

- Character voice IDs are placeholders until the final Inworld mappings are supplied.
- Voice synthesis is presentation; readable subtitles remain the canonical dialogue.
- Spoken player answers require speech-to-text in addition to NPC text-to-speech.
- Every spoken interaction must also support keyboard, text, or selection input.

## Three-act direction

### Chapter 1 — The Lost Knowledge Civilization

Physics and engineering. Asteria restores its main drive by diagnosing an open power circuit, studying candidate materials, and selecting a suitable repair assembly.

### Chapter 2 — The Planet's Secret

Chemistry and biology. Asteria travels to a real Solar System destination chosen to match the final learning goals. Any artificial ecosystem or ancient machinery is clearly fictional and layered onto reviewed facts about that body.

### Chapter 3 — The Final Memory

AI and human knowledge. Asteria restores the Science Core and discovers why the archive was created: AI exists to help people preserve understanding and continue discovering, not to replace scientists.

## Open narrative questions

- What caused the central memory failure, and was it accidental?
- Is the ancient civilization human, non-human, or a misunderstood archive attribution?
- Which real destination best serves Chapter 2's exact curriculum?
- Why is AURA still operating when the central system is not?
- What age range and reading level should dialogue target?
- Which characters receive cloned voices, and what are their final Inworld voice IDs?

Open questions are not canon until explicitly resolved.
